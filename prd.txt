1. Executive Summary
Artiva is a mobile-responsive web platform that connects homeowners and small contractors in Abuja with verified, available artisans for urgent home repairs. It addresses a two-sided market failure: clients cannot find a reliable artisan fast enough during an emergency, and skilled artisans cannot get consistent, well-paying leads.
The platform's core value proposition is speed and protection: a verified artisan is matched in under 30 minutes, with the client's payment held in escrow until the job is confirmed complete. It is not a directory — it is a trust-and-urgency marketplace. All negotiation, messaging, and transactions happen inside the app; there is no external WhatsApp or phone hand-off.
This PRD covers the MVP scope only: the minimum set of features required to prove the core loop end-to-end, generate real revenue, and demonstrate Product-Market Fit within a single beachhead estate — Life Camp (NTA Estate), Abuja.
As of v1.9, the client's platform fee and job value are collected in a single consolidated checkout rather than two separate payments, and — for jobs that require materials — the artisan never handles client cash for supplies. Instead, the artisan submits a supplier proforma invoice and the platform pays the supplier directly from the escrowed funds. Both changes reduce friction and reduce fraud risk without changing the underlying revenue model.

2. Problem Statement
2.1 The Problem
Nigeria's informal skilled labour market is fragmented, unregulated, and high-risk for both sides of the transaction. When a homeowner needs an urgent repair, the existing alternatives fail consistently in the one moment clients are most willing to pay for certainty.
The problem manifests as three connected pain points:

2.2 The Insight
The real competitor is not another app. It is the estate WhatsApp group and the usual guy's phone number. Those alternatives work adequately when there is time to vet someone. They fail completely in the urgent moment — exactly when clients are most willing to pay for certainty.
Speed without trust is what WhatsApp groups already offer. Trust without speed is what existing artisan directories already own. The combination — verified and available within 30 minutes, with all communication and money protected inside the platform — is the gap nobody currently owns.

7. Functional Requirements
7.1 Authentication & Artisan Onboarding
Phone OTP via Firebase Auth — no email required
OTP requests rate-limited to 3 per phone number per hour; 24-hour lockout after 5 failed attempts
Session token required on every authenticated request
Admin access controlled by environment-variable admin UID — not a role field in the database
Artisan 3-step signup: (1) phone OTP + name/experience/location, (2) trade + specific services, (3) NIN + ID document + 3-5 work photos + NDPR consent
NIN is stored securely for verification only — never shown to clients. Accepted ID documents: NIN Slip, Driver's Licence, Voter's Card, International Passport
Google/Apple continue buttons are shown on signup for UX polish; Firebase phone OTP remains the only functional auth path for MVP (flagged for team review — full OAuth is a post-MVP add if time allows)
7.2 Matching Engine
Hard filter: trade = job.trade AND available = true AND verified = true
Sort: distance ascending (closest first)
Tiebreaker: completed_jobs descending, then reputation_score descending (null = sorted last)
Zero-result handling: return empty list with empty-state trigger; never return unverified artisans
Concentration fix: rotation weight applied to prevent the same artisan dominating consecutive matches in the same estate area
Note: The matching engine is rule-based by design. A learned ranking model is scoped for Q3 once 100–150 real completed jobs provide sufficient training signal.
Priority Score Algorithm (v1)
priority_score = (avg_rating × 0.40) + (min(completed_jobs, 50) / 50 × 0.30) + (response_speed_score × 0.20) + (verification_bonus × 0.10)
response_speed_score: 1.0 if avg response < 30 min; 0.5 if < 2 hours; 0.2 if < 24 hours; 0.0 otherwise
verification_bonus: 1.0 if verified = true; 0.3 if verified = pending

7.3 Escrow & Consolidated Payment Flow (v1.9)
Artiva operates an escrow-lite model. As of v1.9, the client's job value and ₦500 platform fee are collected in a single consolidated Paystack transaction at checkout — not two separate payments — and the entire amount is held by the platform until the job is confirmed complete. From that point, funds follow one of two release paths: a standard labour release on Mark Complete, or a direct-to-supplier release when the job requires materials (Section 7.6).
Step
Action
System Behaviour
1
Client and artisan agree a price in-app, then client taps the consolidated Checkout — job value + ₦500 fee
Single Paystack transaction initialised server-side for the combined amount, itemized as job_value and platform_match_fee; amounts locked in transaction document; no-response timer started (4-hour countdown)
2
Client completes Paystack checkout
Paystack fires webhook to platform on success
3
Platform receives webhook
Signature (HMAC) verified; escrow_status updated to HELD; in-app chat thread opened between client and artisan
4a — Standard labour release
Client taps Mark Complete
Authorization checked; commission calculated server-side (job_value × 0.10); artisan_net_labor (job_value minus commission) released to artisan; escrow_status updated to RELEASED; no-response timer cancelled
4b — Material path
Artisan submits a verified supplier proforma invoice (Section 7.6)
Platform pays the supplier directly from escrow for the invoiced amount; escrow_status updated to DISBURSED_PARTIAL; remaining balance still held for the labour release on Mark Complete
4c — No-response path
Artisan sends no in-app message within 4 hours of match
Platform initiates automatic Paystack refund to client for the full held amount; escrow_status updated to REFUNDED; artisan profile receives a non-response flag; admin notified; client sees an in-app message explaining the refund
5
Rating submitted
1–5 star value stored; reputation_score recalculated; duplicate submission rejected with 409
6
Payment failure
Failed payment screen rendered; retry re-initialises the same consolidated payment; job remains open
Note: Definition of "contact": the first message sent by the artisan inside the Artiva chat thread. Opening the app alone does not count. The 4-hour window is deliberate: short enough to protect the client from being left waiting, long enough to account for artisans who are mid-job when a lead arrives. The window is enforced by a server-side timer (Firebase scheduled function), not dependent on client action. Consolidating the checkout in v1.9 does not change this — the timer still starts at the same point in the flow, now step 1.

7.6 Direct Material Supplier Procurement Protocol (v1.9)
To remove the risk of material funds being misappropriated between client payment and material purchase, v1.9 introduces a strict direct-to-supplier payout protocol for any job that requires materials.
Zero cash to artisan for materials: artisans are never given raw cash or a partial escrow advance to buy materials themselves
Proforma invoice upload: when a job needs materials, the artisan uploads an official proforma invoice from a named supplier through the in-app proforma module
Platform verification & disbursement: admin reviews the invoice in the proforma queue (Section 6.3, AD-005) and, on approval, the platform pays the supplier directly from escrow — never through the artisan's account
Quality control: this protects the client's payment and keeps material sourcing auditable, without adding a supplier-facing marketplace to the MVP (that remains Phase 2 — see Section 5.2)

12.3 Why Commission Cannot Be Skipped
The client pays the full job value plus the match fee at booking. The platform holds the entire amount in escrow. On Mark Complete, the platform releases the artisan's share (job value minus 10%) and retains the commission. The artisan cannot receive their share without the job being marked complete, and the commission is never invoiced separately — it is retained automatically from funds already held.
